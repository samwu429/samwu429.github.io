# Course brief — CSC105H5 F (Fall 2026), Artificial Intelligence Literacy

University of Toronto Mississauga, instructor Joshua Jung. Site `topphi.com/csc105h5`, pack `study/courses/csc105h5`. Read `study/tools/content-spec.md` and `study/tools/review-spec.md` first; this brief adds what is specific to CSC105H5.

## 1. Units

- Lectures `lec01`…`lec07` (`"kind": "lecture"`) and labs `lab01`…`lab05` (`"kind": "lab"`).
- Lectures have 10–18 sections, labs 4–8 sections.
- Lab lessons: the first section explains the lab's goal, then what you did step by step, then the concepts it practiced (with links in words to the lecture, e.g. "对应 Lecture 3 的 minimax"), then takeaways.

## 2. Tests

- Tests are "a mix of multiple choice and long-answer questions (no coding)".
- Test 1 was 2026-10-06; **Test 2 is 2026-11-10** (1 hour, in class, 15%); **Final exam** (2 hours, covers all material, 40%, must get >= 40% on it to pass). Do NOT guess which lectures each test covers.

## 3. Proper names (do not translate)

Python, PencilCode, Teachable Machine, Sokoban, Connect Four, Hua Rong Dao (华容道 may be added), COMPAS, Russell and Norvig, Turing Test (图灵测试（**Turing Test**）).

## 4. Notation to keep exactly

- Worked numbers to reproduce, for example the lec05 MSE example 83.42 / 7 ≈ 11.92 and the lec06 Precision = 28/40 = 70%.
- lec05 regression uses `y = w0 + w1x1`, target `t`, `error = y − t`.
- lec06 confusion matrix has PREDICTED rows and ACTUAL columns (TP top-left, FP top-right, FN bottom-left, TN bottom-right).
- lab05 model cards lay their confusion matrices out the other way: ACTUAL rows × PREDICTED columns.
- minimax uses ▲ (our turn, maximize) and ▼ (opponent, minimize); rewards win +100 / loss −100 / draw 0.
- Typical confusions to teach as pitfalls: precision vs recall, validation vs test set.

## 5. Question and glossary targets

- Per lecture aim for **28–40 questions**: ~14–18 `mcq`, ~4–6 `tf`, ~2–5 `multi`, `calc` wherever numbers appear in the lecture (minimax values, accuracy/error, MSE, precision/recall/F1, rates…), ~4–6 `short`, ~2–4 `long`. Labs: 8–14 questions.
- Glossary: 20–45 terms per lecture, 6–20 per lab.

## 6. Review files

- `cheatsheet.json`: one unit per lecture (`lec01`…`lec07`), 10–18 points each; labs only where they add exam-relevant facts (optional units `lab01`…`lab05` with 3–6 points). Confusables such as validation vs test set, classification vs regression, disparate treatment vs disparate impact, symbolic AI vs pure algorithm, parameter vs hyperparameter.
- `exam-toolkit.json` templates map to the real kinds of CSC105 long-answer questions: choose an AI method for a problem (use the big table of properties), explain how an algorithm works (minimax, exhaustive search, gradient descent), compute and interpret metrics (accuracy, precision/recall/F1, MSE), explain overfitting and the role of validation/test sets, analyse fairness (metrics, trade-offs, stakeholders, disparate treatment/impact), ethics (alignment, privacy, copyright, environmental cost, hallucination), explain how an LLM produces a response (tokenization, embeddings, attention, next token, temperature), neural network basics.
- `course-overview.json`: `branches` are the three kinds of AI used throughout the course (symbolic/algorithmic, machine learning, LLMs/generative), plus ethics as a thread across all of them. `studyPlans`: (1) `test2` — a 13-day plan from Oct 28 to Nov 10 (do not claim a scope; tell the learner to confirm the scope on Quercus/Piazza and to prioritize what was taught since Test 1); (2) `final` — a plan for the final exam (all material).
